A **testimony** is something a resident said happened to them. An **observation** is a problem that
is on file without that — noticed while reading the material back, inferred from an account that
was about something else, or simply raised in a working session because somebody thought of it.

Observations exist because the alternative was worse. Until now the only door into this project
was a dictated account, and a problem nobody happened to dictate had nowhere to go — so it either
got smuggled into a policy area it did not belong in, or it was lost. Both are worse than writing
it down and being honest about its standing.

The price of the door is **lineage**. Every observation states, in its own frontmatter and in its
opening line, how it came to be here. A problem with a weak provenance that says so is usable. A
problem with a weak provenance that does not say so contaminates everything downstream of it.

## Rules

1. **`origin` is mandatory**, and it is a sentence, not a category. "Derived from the 10 August
   account, which mentions the heat twice without ever making it the subject" is an origin.
   "Session" is not.
2. **An observation is not evidence of anything.** A testimony is evidence of experience — weak
   evidence of prevalence, but real evidence that a thing happened to somebody. An observation is
   evidence only that the problem occurred to someone. It cannot be cited, quoted as experience,
   or counted.
3. **An observation does not open a policy area.** Areas still open on testimony. An observation
   can sit in the queue indefinitely, and most will. What it can do is tell you which testimony is
   worth going out and getting.
4. **Promotion is one-directional and it is recorded.** If testimony later arrives that covers an
   observation, the area opens on the testimony, and the observation is marked promoted with a
   link. The observation is never rewritten into a testimony — that would launder its provenance,
   which is the one thing this document class exists to prevent.
5. **The powers gate applies unchanged.** Nothing here proposes anything. Several of these note
   which class a lever would probably fall into; those are hypotheses written down so they can be
   checked, and under [the evidence rules](../method/evidence-rules.md) they are `[UNVERIFIED]`
   like anything else.

## Why these, and not fifty

An observation costs almost nothing to write, which is exactly the danger: a list of every
conceivable municipal shortcoming is not a platform, it is a mood. The ones below were kept to two
kinds.

- **Things the account already contains but never made the subject** — mentioned in passing, as
  texture around a different grievance. These have the strongest lineage of anything here, because
  the words exist on file.
- **Things the account's own vantage point would predictably miss**, identified by reading
  [whose account this is](../context/whose-account.md) and asking what a household in that
  position sees so constantly that it stops registering as a problem.

Anything that was merely a good idea about cities was left out.
