In the account the heat is scenery. It is the thing that made a bad afternoon worse, mentioned
once, on the way to a complaint about bus frequency. Nobody said the city should do anything about
it, and this document should not pretend otherwise.

The reframing is this: **a resident experiences heat as weather, but a good deal of what makes
heat bearable in a street is built rather than meteorological.** Shade over the stop, a tree over
the pavement, the surface underfoot, whether the walk is in sun the whole way. Those are choices
somebody made, and in most cities the somebody is the municipality.

That makes it worth a line here for one specific reason: it is the sort of thing residents
systematically fail to attribute to City Hall — which is the exact inverse of this project's
recurring finding, and therefore interesting. The pipeline is built to catch grievances aimed at
the wrong body. This is a candidate for the opposite error: a municipal responsibility that
nobody thinks to blame anyone for.

## Where it touches what is already on file

- **[Public transport](../policy/public-transport/index.md)** already carries "no shade while you
  wait" as part of its thesis, and the area tracker names bus stop ownership, shelters and shade
  as the likeliest class-A finding in the area — verification-debt item 21 in [tasks](../tasks.md).
  That is the strongest existing hook, and it is the reason this observation is filed against
  `public-transport` rather than as a candidate area of its own.
- **[Construction disruption](../policy/construction-disruption/index.md)** — a diverted pedestrian
  route is generally a longer, more exposed one, and temporary routes rarely inherit whatever shade
  the original had.
- **[Environmental quality](../policy/environmental-quality/index.md)** — which currently rests on
  two sentences and is the thinnest area in the project. If heat and the street environment belong
  anywhere as a subject rather than a detail, it is probably here, and it would roughly double the
  area's substance.

## Honest assessment

This is the weakest item in this folder and it is filed accordingly. It has one sentence of
lineage, it was not offered as a grievance, and it may be a case of a policy process finding a
problem because it was looking for one. It is here because the shelter question was already
independently identified as the area's best class-A candidate, and because it costs nothing to
keep a note that says *ask about this next time*.

If no future testimony raises it, it should be deleted rather than quietly promoted.
