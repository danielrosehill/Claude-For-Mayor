The one subject the account returns to unprompted, from opposite directions, and the one it never
frames as a complaint about City Hall — which is probably why it has no policy area.

**The constraint side**, from the opening dictation: every job search in Israel begins with where
you are based, and Jerusalem is the answer that produces an audible sigh. Fewer options, lower
pay, and a cost of living close to the centre's. Ten years of that is described as a limiting
factor on a professional life, not as a municipal failing.
[Whose account this is](../context/whose-account.md).

**The opportunity side**, from the fourth dictation: fibre penetration is good, fast trains
connect the city to Tel Aviv and the centre, and the Tel Aviv-versus-Jerusalem framing is outdated
in a way that remote work makes concrete. The city is not a backwater; it is well connected to
government and to the coast, and it has institutions — government, think tanks, policy — that
another city of its size would not have.
[What is good about the city](../context/what-works.md).

Put together, those are a thesis rather than two anecdotes: **the city's economic problem may be
one of composition rather than of distance**, and the strategy it has publicly bet on — tourism,
diplomatic events, embassy openings — is the one most exposed to things nobody in the building
controls. That last point is already recorded, in
[capital priorities](../policy/capital-priorities/index.md), as the mirror of the tourism
objection.

## Why it has not been opened

[Tasks](../tasks.md) gives the reason and it still holds: eight areas already sit behind an unrun
jurisdiction gate, and adding a ninth makes the ratio worse rather than better. There is a second
reason that matters more.

**It is not clear the municipality holds a lever over an employment market at all.** That is not a
rhetorical hedge — it is the exact question `/map-powers` exists to answer, and this is the area
where a wrong answer would be most expensive. A mayoral platform with an economy chapter that
turns out to be class D throughout would be the most conventional thing about it, and the most
dishonest.

The plausible municipal hooks, all `[UNVERIFIED]` and all written down here only so they can be
tested rather than assumed:

| Candidate lever | Why it might be municipal | Expected class |
|---|---|---|
| Business licensing conditions and the time a licence takes | Licensing is administered locally | A, on the administration if not the substance |
| Arnona classification for commercial and office property, and any relief power | The classification schedule is the city's; the rate is not | B at best |
| Municipal procurement — what the city buys, from whom, and on what terms | Tender conditions are the clearest class-A instrument in the doctrine | A |
| Commercial floorspace in planning outcomes | Bears directly on what Talpiot is losing; see [small businesses](../policy/small-businesses/index.md) | B or C |
| Anything resembling inward investment or "attracting employers" | The standard municipal promise, and the one most likely to be theatre | Expect D, or A-but-worthless |

## What would open it

A first-person account of trying to work, hire, or run something here — not the account already on
file, which is about the experience of *living* here with that market in the background. Someone
who has tried to get a business licence, or who employs people in the city, or who took the pay
cut and can say what it cost. That is testimony. This is not.
