The second dictation ends mid-sentence on its sharpest point: dissatisfaction with the daily
reality of living here is *contained*, because it happens in groups, offline, in Hebrew.
[The argument the city is having](../context/the-ideological-agenda.md) records that as a claim
about political containment, and [tasks](../tasks.md) treats it as a methodological warning —
an English-language repository kept by an anglophone immigrant will systematically miss the
evidence that would confirm or refute most of what is on file. Item 8 of the next actions is,
bluntly, *go and read the Hebrew*.

Both of those are right. This observation is the third reading of the same sentence.

**If the city's information ecosystem is Hebrew and offline, then everyone whose Hebrew is not up
to it is outside it** — and that is not a small group in Jerusalem. It includes recent olim, it
includes long-settled immigrant households like the one that gave this account, and, in a very
different way and with very different politics attached, it includes a large Arabic-speaking
population whose relationship to municipal information this project has said nothing about and is
in no position to characterise.

## Why this is filed carefully, and narrowly

Language provision in Jerusalem is not a neutral administrative question, and this repository has
no testimony from anyone for whom it is the central issue. Two constraints follow, and they are
binding.

1. **No claim is made here about Arabic-language municipal services**, in either direction. The
   project has no evidence, and the subject deserves testimony from people it actually affects
   rather than inference from an English-speaking household's inconvenience. Recorded as a gap,
   not filled with a guess.
2. **The English case is not a claim of unfairness.** A Hebrew-speaking country running a
   Hebrew-speaking municipality is not a grievance. The narrow question is whether *operational*
   information — what road is closed, when the noise stops, how to register an arnona change of
   holder, when a committee meets — reaches people the city knows are there.

## The thin end that is actually testable

The strongest version of this is not about language rights at all. It is that
[the consultation failure](./2026-08-10-consultation-and-objection.md) and this observation are the
same failure at different depths: if the works notice does not exist, language is irrelevant; if it
exists only in Hebrew, then a resident who reports "there was no notice" and a municipality that
reports "we published a notice" can both be telling the truth.

That is a checkable proposition and it changes what the consultation finding means, so it is worth
holding.

## What would open it

Testimony from someone who missed something that mattered because of where it was published, and
can name the thing they missed. Nobody has given that account. Until they do, this is a question,
not a plank.
