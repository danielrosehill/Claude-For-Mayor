Opened by [A bridge I have never used](../../testimonies/2026-08-10-capital-priorities.md),
10 August 2026.

**The thesis, provisionally.** The charge is not that tourism infrastructure is worthless. It is
that the *allocation* is wrong: capital directed at a transitory population, with a marginal
benefit per resident-year that is very low, while investments that would measurably improve daily
life go unmade. Stated that way it is an arithmetic claim, and arithmetic claims can be settled.

Two things have to survive contact with the evidence before any of it can be published. First,
**whose capital is it** — if the prestige projects are nationally funded or delivered by a
development authority, then "spend it on pavements instead" was very likely never an option, and
the honest proposal is about what the city *asks for*, not what it spends. Second, **is the
counterfactual real** — earmarked money usually cannot be moved, and an opportunity-cost argument
against money that was never fungible is rhetoric rather than analysis.

If both survive, this becomes the area with the most direct bearing on every other one, because it
is where the money for them would come from.

Nothing below stage 1 has been done.

### Attributions to test

| # | The account assumes | To test |
|:-:|---|---|
| 1 | The municipality chose these projects | Many are likely national or development-authority led. Expect **C** or **D** on delivery — at which point the real question becomes what the city asked for and what it declined to ask for, which is still a municipal question |
| 2 | The same capital could have gone to daily-life infrastructure | The load-bearing assumption of the entire area. Earmarked funding usually cannot be moved. If the counterfactual fails, the area survives only as an advocacy argument about what the city requests |
| 3 | "The municipality trumpets these around the world" | Directly checkable against its own published material and press releases. Quote it; do not characterise it |
| 4 | Residents do not benefit | An empirical claim about usage and local employment. The testimony offers one data point — the author has never used the walkway — which is evidence of experience and not of prevalence |
| 5 | Banking on tourism is strategically bad | An economic judgement, not a jurisdictional one. It needs the volatility data in fact 4 and a named alternative development strategy, or it stays an opinion and is labelled as one |
