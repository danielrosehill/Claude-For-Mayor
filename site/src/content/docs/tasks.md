The honest state of the project, kept in public. Verification debt is listed here rather than
quietly carried, because a platform that argues from sourced evidence does not get to hide its
unsourced parts.

## Where it stands

The **method is built and the content has just started**. The pipeline, the plugin, the powers
doctrine and the context scaffold exist. On 10 August 2026 the first testimony landed — a single
dictated account, given in two sittings, which opened seven areas at once. All seven sit at stage
1, and under our own rules nothing in them may be proposed until the jurisdiction gate has been
run.

Seven areas and zero jurisdiction maps is the wrong ratio, and it is the main thing to fix. The
account was offered as a scattergun list and it was taken at face value: an area opens when there
is testimony behind it, so seven opened. The cost is that the queue in front of `/map-powers` is
now four times longer than it was, and some of those areas rest on two or three sentences.

## Next actions

**Blocking everything:**

1. **Run `/map-powers` on the seven open areas.** Nothing may be researched, drafted or proposed
   in any of them until the jurisdiction gate has been cleared. Suggested order:

   1. **construction-disruption** — its attributions are the most likely to be wrong, which makes
      it the sharpest first test of the skill.
   2. **construction-noise** — shares the permit question with the one above, so running them
      together does the statutory reading once.
   3. **renting** — the clearest candidate for an unused class-A power.
   4. **city-centre-businesses** — shares the arnona constraint with renting; same argument.
   5. **housing-supply** — depends most on the standing powers primer being done.
   6. **capital-priorities** — the jurisdiction question and the evidence question are the same
      question here, so it is the most expensive of the seven.
   7. **environmental-quality** — last on purpose. A **D** on the headline grievance is a live
      possibility, and finding that out cheaply is what the gate is for.

**Runnable now, independent of the threads:**

2. **Build the verified powers primer** — items 1, 2, 6 and 7 above. This is the largest piece of
   standing research in the project and every policy area will draw on it, so doing it once
   properly beats doing it six times partially.
3. **Research the City Hall baseline** — the coalition as constituted, portfolio and committee
   allocation, any changes since February 2024, and the administration's record. Coalition
   agreements first; they are the highest-value document in that section.
4. **Re-verify the 2024 results** against the Ministry of the Interior, and settle the next
   election date.

**Deferred:**

5. Cover image and Open Graph image for the site — the landing page and social cards currently
   have neither.
6. First run of `/map-powers` will be the plugin's real test. Expect the command and the skill to
   need revision afterwards; that revision is part of the experiment, not a defect.
7. Broaden the testimony base. Seven areas resting on one household is the project's biggest
   methodological weakness right now, and no amount of stage-2 research fully fixes it. It got
   four times worse on 10 August, not better.
8. **Go and read the Hebrew.** The account's own closing point is that dissatisfaction here is
   contained in offline, Hebrew-language spaces —
   [the argument the city is having](./context/the-ideological-agenda.md). An English-language
   repository kept by an anglophone immigrant will systematically miss exactly the evidence that
   would confirm or refute most of what is above. Neighbourhood groups, Hebrew local media,
   community administration minutes and the municipality's own Hebrew publications are where
   stage 2 has to go.
9. Recover the end of the 10 August account. The transcript stops mid-sentence on the
   containment point; whatever followed was not captured.

## Candidate threads

Not commitments, and not chosen — these are areas where a Jerusalem resident plausibly has a
first-person account, listed so the blank page is less blank. The user picks; an area only opens
when there is testimony behind it.

Cleanliness and refuse collection · street and pavement condition away from the works · parking
and enforcement · the light rail and bus network as experienced rather than as planned · building
permits and the local planning committee · parks, playgrounds and shade · the community
administrations · what the municipality publishes and what it does not · services in
neighbourhoods that get less of them · animal control · signage and wayfinding · what happens when
you call the municipal hotline · **Shabbat and public space** — named in the account as the
argument the city is actually having, and deliberately not opened as a policy area on the strength
of that; it opens if there is testimony about it rather than about its salience · **Jerusalem as
an economic and professional constraint** — fewer jobs, lower pay, a cost of living close to the
centre's; raised in the [opening account](./context/whose-account.md) but not yet opened, because
it is not yet clear the municipality holds a lever over it.

The ones worth doing are the ones where the resident's assumption about who is responsible turns
out to be wrong, or where a class-A power has been sitting unused. Neither is knowable in advance
of the testimony.
