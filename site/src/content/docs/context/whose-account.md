A platform assembled from [testimony](../testimonies/index.md) inherits the limits of whoever
gave it. Disclosing the vantage point is not throat-clearing here — it is the same discipline as
citing a source, and it is what makes "this account is unrepresentative of the city" a checkable
objection rather than a vague one.

## How I ended up here

I moved to Israel from Ireland in 2015 and have lived in Jerusalem the entire time. I never
intended to come to Jerusalem — maybe like most people. My aliyah interview was allocated by
date, and Jerusalem was the option that came with it. That is how I got here. Then I met my now
wife in Jerusalem, and I never left: the relationship, friends, and my wife's work being
substantially in the centre of the city.

I have always found Jerusalem frustrating. Professionally it is a real limiting factor. Every
time I have looked for work in Israel, the first question is where you are based, and you can
almost hear the sigh when you say Jerusalem. Fewer options, lower pay — and a cost of living
almost as high as the centre of the country.

And underneath all of that, despite the setbacks, I have a persistent sense that being here is
not accidental, and that some part of what I am supposed to do is improve the city. Not in a
grandiose way. More like a part of a life's mission that I did not choose and have stopped
arguing with.

This project is what that turns into if you refuse to just complain.

## Why the news about Jerusalem is not what this is about

I read the news about Jerusalem and mostly it is disappointment. What gets the headlines is
endless material about the Temple Mount and political violence, and it feeds a public image of
Jerusalem as the ultimate place of division, strife and conflict.

I do not think anything can change that, and this platform does not try to. Those optics are a
product of a media environment in which that is what grabs attention. But **there are a great
many things that are within the city's control**, and they get almost none of that attention.
Those are the things this project is about — which is also, not coincidentally, exactly where
[the powers doctrine](../method/powers-doctrine.md) says a mayoral platform ought to spend its
effort.

## What this vantage point cannot see

Stated plainly, so that it can be corrected. This account comes from a household that is:

- **A renter, continuously, for ten years** — never an owner, never a landlord. Nearly every
  instinct in [the renting testimony](../testimonies/2026-08-10-renting.md) is a renter's
  instinct.
- **Anglophone and an immigrant.** Interactions with the municipality are shaped by operating in
  a second language and by not having grown up inside Israeli bureaucratic norms. Some of what
  reads as municipal dysfunction may be unfamiliarity, and the research has to allow for that.
- **In the centre and west of the city**, not East Jerusalem, and not inside the Haredi
  neighbourhoods. Under the
  [neighbourhood discipline](../method/evidence-rules.md) this project holds itself to, a
  city-wide claim resting only on this vantage point is not a city-wide claim.
- **A young family with a small child** — which sharpens some things, notably pavements,
  pushchair access and construction routing, and makes others invisible.

Where a testimony's reach runs out, the fix is data, comparator cities, and the city's own
published record — not a more strongly worded testimony.

The language point turns out to be the sharpest of the four. The account's own closing observation
is that dissatisfaction with daily life in Jerusalem is largely contained, because it happens
offline and in Hebrew — which means an English-language project run from this vantage point will
systematically under-sample exactly the evidence it most needs.
[The argument the city is having](./the-ideological-agenda.md) sets that out, and it is a research
obligation rather than a disclaimer.

## The thread that is not yet a policy area

One strand of the account has no area open behind it: **Jerusalem as an economic and
professional constraint** — fewer jobs, lower pay, and a cost of living close to the centre's.
It is real and it is felt, but a municipality's levers over an employment market are mostly
indirect, and until that is mapped it is not clear whether there is a proposal here or only a
grievance. It is listed with the other
[candidate threads](../tasks.md) and it opens if and when there is a testimony and a lever
behind it.
