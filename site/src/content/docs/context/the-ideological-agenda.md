This page records a framing claim from the [opening account](./whose-account.md), separately from
the policy areas, because it is not a grievance with a lever behind it. It is a claim about what
municipal politics in Jerusalem is *about* — and it shapes which areas this project opens and what
counts as evidence for them.

It is unverified. It is written down here so that it can be attacked.

## The claim

Debates in Jerusalem are so tied up in the ideology of the city. Shabbat, and access on Shabbat.
As I write this we are watching a storm in a teacup — a protest, week after week, over a coffee
shop that opened.

That, more than anything else, typifies the experience of governance in this city for the people
who live here.

And it is not what improves daily life. What improves the daily experience of living here is, to a
good extent, the quality of the environment, and the ordinary operational competence of the
municipality: whether you can find out which road is closed, whether the excavation next door has
an end date, whether the shops on your street are still open.

## What this is not saying

The ideological questions are real, they are genuinely contested, and a council in which the
Haredi lists hold 16 of 31 seats between them means no platform gets to pretend otherwise — see
[City Hall](./city-hall/index.md). People who care intensely about Shabbat in public space are not
mistaken about their own preferences, and a platform that treats them as a distraction has
misunderstood the city it wants to govern.

The claim is narrower and it is this: **the loudest municipal argument is not the one with the
largest effect on most residents' days.** That is a proposition about salience versus effect, and
it is checkable. It sits alongside — not instead of — the
[powers doctrine](../method/powers-doctrine.md), which says to spend effort where the city actually
holds levers. Together they define where this project looks: things that materially affect daily
life, that the municipality can actually do something about, and that nobody is arguing about.

The obvious failure mode is stating a preference and calling it a finding. If the evidence says
residents rank the ideological questions above the operational ones, this page is wrong and the
platform's emphasis is wrong with it.

## Why the dissatisfaction stays invisible

The dissatisfaction with the daily reality of life in the city is largely contained, because it
happens in groups offline, and it happens in Hebrew.

*(The account is cut off at this point in the recording; what followed was not captured.)*

That has a consequence for this project specifically, and it is not a small one. An
English-language repository, kept by an anglophone immigrant, will systematically under-sample
exactly the material that would prove or disprove everything on this page. Neighbourhood WhatsApp
and Facebook groups, Hebrew-language local media, community administration meetings and the
municipality's own Hebrew publications are where this evidence lives. Stage 2 research has to go
there, and "I could not find it in English" is not a finding.

This is a sharper version of the limitation already recorded in
[whose account this is](./whose-account.md).
