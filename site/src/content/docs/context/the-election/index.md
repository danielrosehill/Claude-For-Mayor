## How the city is elected

Israeli local authorities elect a mayor and a council on the same day, on **two separate
ballots** — one for the head of the authority, one for the council list. The two are independent,
so a mayor can be elected with a large personal majority while his own list holds only a handful
of council seats, and in Jerusalem that is exactly what happens.

Other features of the system that bear on strategy:

- **The mayor is directly elected**, with a **runoff between the top two** where no candidate
  clears 40% in the first round.
- **The council is proportional**, allocated by largest remainder across the lists that clear the
  threshold.
- **Elections are held every five years**, and are national — every municipality, local council
  and regional council goes to the polls on the same day.
- **Election day is a paid day off work**, under a 2018 change.
- The Knesset can and does **legislate postponements**: the 2023 round was pushed from 31 October
  2023 to 30 January 2024 because of the war, and then again to 27 February 2024 because so many
  candidates were serving as reservists.

**In Jerusalem specifically:** Palestinian residents of East Jerusalem hold permanent residency
rather than citizenship, which means they **may vote in the municipal election** — though not in
Knesset elections. Their participation has historically been negligible, and that fact shapes the
arithmetic of every city-wide policy question. Establishing the actual figures, rather than the
commonly repeated ones, is an open task.

## What the result implies for a platform

Three things, and they set the strategic frame for everything in this repository:

1. **A two-thirds majority of the electorate did not vote.** 66% abstention on a mayoral contest
   decided by 81–19 is the central political fact of the city. Any platform aimed at people who
   already vote is aimed at a third of Jerusalem.
2. **The mayor's own list holds 2 of 31 seats.** Mayoral authority in Jerusalem runs through
   coalition-building with the Haredi lists — Degel HaTorah, Shas, Agudat Yisrael and Bnei Torah
   held 16 of 31 seats between them in 2024, a bare majority on their own. Any proposal that
   needs a council vote needs an answer to that arithmetic, and
   [the council arithmetic check](../../platform/index.md) in stage 4 exists for it.
3. **A very large share of residents are outside the system entirely.** Between the East
   Jerusalem franchise question and general abstention, a platform written only against the
   revealed preferences of 2024 voters would be a platform for a minority of the city.

## The next election

Under the five-year cycle, the next round falls due in **2028**. The precise statutory date — and
whether the 2024 postponements shifted the cycle or left it anchored — is **not yet verified
here**, and is an open task. Given the Knesset's demonstrated willingness to move the date by
legislation, treat any date as provisional until confirmed against the Local Authorities
(Elections) Law.

This project does not depend on the date. It is not a campaign, and its timetable is set by the
evidence rather than by a poll.
